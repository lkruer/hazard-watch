What this is

A free, open, location-based status board for the natural hazards that can actually be anticipated: rain-triggered landslides, fire-danger conditions, drought, and river levels. Pick a monitored spot and you get one color, one plain sentence, and the numbers behind them — updated daily from public data, built to work on cheap phones and weak connections.

It does not predict earthquakes or tsunamis. Nothing can; a confident-looking number there would be a lie, so we don't show one.

How the scores work

Everything is compared to that location's own history, never an absolute threshold — 40 mm of rain is a wet week in Oregon and a catastrophe in the Atacama. "98th percentile" means: for this place, at this time of year, conditions like today happened only 2% of the time in the last two decades. Slope danger comes from models trained on ~55,000 recorded landslides across four continents; fire danger was checked against 49,000 real fires in two countries; drought agrees with expert US drought maps ~80% of the time; river levels were validated against real river gauges.

Honesty rules

  • Every model's accuracy — including its misses — is measured on data it never saw, and published in the decision log.
  • When two independent weather sources disagree about a place, its rain-based scores are withheld, not served wrong. You'll see "data uncertain" with the reason.
  • A place with weak data still gets shown — with a lower confidence tier, never with fake precision.
  • This is context, not an official warning system. Always follow local authorities.

Limits, stated plainly

  • Weather-based scores lag ~3 days behind real time (source latency).
  • Wind-driven fire events are under-detected — coarse daily wind data can't see downslope windstorms. Measured, documented.
  • Dam-regulated rivers (e.g. the Columbia) follow turbine schedules no natural-flow model can know.
  • Landslide types with no surface signature (deep-seated failures like Oso 2014) need geology data that isn't openly available at usable resolution yet.

Open everything

Code, models, validations, and the full decision history live at github.com/lkruer/hazard-watch. Every input dataset is public; every accuracy claim is reproducible from the repository.
